# Science, evidence, classical sources and explicit theoretical tensions

**Work from the original primary publications when available**, not copied book chapters or uncited paraphrase. The Skill contains **index summaries, not licensed copies of full books**.

| Reference and knowledge type | Useful claim/decision | Evidence level and constraints | Main reconciliation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graham, *The Intelligent Investor* (value philosophy) | Price ≠ value, margin of safety | Philosophy plus illustrative historical experience; valuations are error-prone | vs Bogle: test user skill, time, fee/tax drag; low-cost index as comparison baseline, not guaranteed optimal |
| Bogle, cost-conscious indexing (practitioner + research) | Costs persist, broad diversification can lower burden | Fees mathematically reduce take-home returns; future index gains not guaranteed | vs ALM: liquid goal reserves must not ride long-run equity risk |
| Markowitz, *Portfolio Selection* (1952, quantitative conditional model) | Covariance drives combined dispersion | Exact algebra given estimates; correlations unstable in crises | vs tail risk: add liquidity/stress, not just variance |
| Sharpe, *Arithmetic of Active Management* (1991) | Aggregate active-vs-market identity before/after costs | Mathematical accounting under consistent market partitions | Does not prove no particular skilled active manager can outperform |
| Modigliani (lifecycle economic model) | Allocate consumption across income/retirement stages | Model assumptions on life, credit, future income | Thaler explains saving behavior deviations; use precommitment but preserve liquidity |
| Fisher, real/nominal interest relation | Match inflation and income currency | Identity when inflation measures and horizons match | Inflation assumption does not forecast future real returns |
| Campbell & Shiller (empirical valuation) | Valuation informative for some horizons | Sample- and specification-dependent, very weak for exact near-term timing | vs Bogle: update scenario ranges, avoid tactical reaction |
| Bengen (US historical withdrawal study) | Initial withdrawal heuristic | Historical US 30y samples, not a risk-free instrument or global law | vs lifecycle: locale/taxes/retirement duration and sequence simulation |
| Kahneman/Tversky, Thaler/Benartzi (behavioral research) | Loss aversion, defaults, savings precommitment | Empirical effects depend on context | Behavior tools require consent, must not deprive user of emergency cash |
| CFP Board financial planning practice standards (professional workflow) | Understand, analyze, develop, present, implement, review | Professional procedure, not local licensure in every jurisdiction | Treat law/licensed boundaries separately from process |
| Taleb, extreme-risk critique (philosophy and risk methods) | Historical normal assumptions omit tail events | Contextual critique, no unique optimal allocation | Complement model-based risk with stress tests and exposure caps |

## Ranking evidence correctly

**Hard binding constraints** (current applicable law, funds owned, legitimate permissions, identity/math checks) → **conditional quantitative models** → **strong or context-dependent empirical research** → **historical analogies** → **investment philosophy/user preference**.

This is not a popularity tally or numerical weighted average of theorists. If two competent economic sources disagree, keep two conditional policies with different predictions and assumptions; document data required to falsify each. Legal conflicts require jurisdiction/treaty analysis, not AI majority vote.
